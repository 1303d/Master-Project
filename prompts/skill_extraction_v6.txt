You extract skill items from one job posting and give each item a type. Return JSON only.

Work in three steps.

STEP 1 - Find every candidate.
Read the whole posting, including the duties. Take every tool, method, ability, field of knowledge, law,
standard, credential, degree and experience requirement that the posting mentions.
Lists hide most items: go through every comma list, slash list, "and/or" list, bullet list and item in
parentheses one by one. "Outlook, Excel, PowerPoint, Word, Teams and SharePoint" -> six items.
"VPC, VPN, SSL, load balancers" -> four items. "hand and power tools" -> "hand tools", "power tools".

STEP 2 - Give each candidate ONE type. Ask first: does the WORKER have to know, use or do it?
- skill: an ability or method the worker must have or perform (financial modeling, phlebotomy, troubleshooting,
  written communication, negotiation, data modeling, Spanish).
- knowledge: a field, law, regulation or standard the worker must know, follow or apply (GAAP, contract law,
  HIPAA, OSHA standards, safety regulations, pharmacology).
- tool: named software, platform, programming language, system or equipment the worker uses (Python, Epic,
  Salesforce, AWS, forklift, infusion pumps).
- credential: licence, certification, clearance or registration (RN license, BLS certification, CPA, bar admission).
- education: degree or diploma.
- experience: years, seniority, or experience in a setting, specialty or industry that names no skill
  ("5+ years", "2 years of ICU experience", "outpatient experience preferred").
- task: a duty that names no tool, method or knowledge area ("answer phones", "file records", "attend meetings",
  "assist patients as needed").
- other: anything that only describes the company, product, clients, industry, setting, culture, benefits or
  schedule, and personality traits ("self-starter", "passionate", "detail-oriented", "dependable").

Context decides the type, not the word:
- "Maintain patient confidentiality per HIPAA" -> HIPAA = knowledge.
  "Build features for our HIPAA-compliant messaging app" -> HIPAA = other.
- "Experience deploying services on AWS" -> AWS = tool. "Our platform runs on AWS" -> AWS = other.
- "Knowledge of pediatric nursing" -> pediatric nursing = knowledge. "3 years of pediatric experience" -> experience.
- "Run sprint ceremonies using Kanban" -> Kanban = skill. "Join us as we adopt Kanban across the company" -> other.
- "Opportunity to learn Tableau" -> other (not asked of the worker). "Tableau preferred" -> tool ("preferred" counts).
- "Follow food safety regulations" -> food safety regulations = knowledge. "Comply with all applicable laws" -> other.
- "AWS certification preferred" -> credential. "AWS certification preferred; experience with AWS" -> credential + AWS = tool.
- "Perform venipuncture" -> skill (a clinical procedure written as a duty is still a skill).
- "Reconcile accounts in QuickBooks" -> QuickBooks = tool. "Build prototypes" -> prototyping = skill.
- "We are a fast-growing fintech" / "leading provider of senior living services" -> other.
Never give skill, knowledge or tool to something only because of the job title, the industry or what this kind
of job usually needs. Business outcomes ("revenue growth", "operational efficiency") are other.

STEP 3 - Write each item and check.
1. One item per skill. "Python and SQL" -> two items. "verbal and written communication" -> "verbal communication",
   "written communication".
2. Use the posting's own wording, short (1-4 words). Keep the full official name of a law, standard or tool.
   Remove filler words: "strong", "excellent", "proficiency in", "knowledge of", "experience with", "skills".
   Never replace a term with a broader, narrower or related concept.
3. Each item once per posting. The same skill repeated with only a time or scope word changed is one item:
   "month-end, quarter-end and year-end close" -> "month-end close".
4. Soft skills as the usual noun: "organized" -> "organization"; "multi-tasking" -> "multitasking";
   "analytical" -> "analytical skills"; "team player" -> "teamwork".
5. Before you answer, re-read the requirements and qualifications and check that no listed tool or technology
   is missing, and that every skill / knowledge / tool item is something the worker must know or use.

For each item, copy "evidence": the shortest exact phrase from the posting that supports it.
A posting may have zero skill items.
