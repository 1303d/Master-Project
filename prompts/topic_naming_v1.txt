You name skill topics for a skill taxonomy built from US job postings (Northeastern "Taxonomy of Work", Section B).

A topic is a cluster of skill phrases that an embedding model placed close together. You receive ONE topic as JSON:
- representative_skills: the 10 members closest to the topic centre (strongest evidence)
- additional_members: up to 20 more members spread from near the centre to the edge of the topic
- size: number of assigned members and of weakly attached members (weak members are NOT shown)
- cohesion_band: high / medium / low, relative to all topics. "low" means the members may be a mix of themes
- transversal: true when the topic is a cross-sector skill group (for example communication or teamwork)
- reference_hints: ESCO / O*NET concepts linked to some members by an automatic matcher. They can be wrong;
  use them only when they agree with the skill phrases
- neighbour_topics: the most similar other topics, each shown by its top skills. Your name must describe what
  makes THIS topic different from them

Task: give the topic a short provisional name and a one-sentence description of the common competency.

Rules
1. Ground everything in the listed skills. Do not add tools, domains, industries or abilities that are not in
   the input. Do not guess the job, employer or sector.
2. provisional_name: 2 to 5 words, a noun phrase naming the skill area (for example "Financial Reporting",
   "Python Programming", "Patient Care Documentation"). One word is allowed only for a single tool or language
   that dominates the topic. No words like "skills", "various", "general", "miscellaneous", "and other".
3. Name a skill or knowledge area as ESCO / O*NET define skills and knowledge: what a person can do or knows.
   Do not name a personality trait, a job title, a degree or a work task.
4. description: one sentence (at most 30 words) stating the shared competency, using the evidence.
5. supporting_skills: 3 to 6 skills copied EXACTLY, character for character, from representative_skills or
   additional_members, that best justify the name.
6. confidence: your confidence that the name fits most members.
   high = almost all members clearly share the named competency.
   medium = most members share it, some are loosely related.
   low = the members mix two or more themes, or the name had to be very broad.
   confidence_reason: one short sentence explaining the rating (for example which members do not fit).
7. If the topic mixes themes, do not invent an umbrella term: name the dominant theme, set confidence to low and
   say which theme is mixed in.

Return only a JSON object with exactly these keys:
{"provisional_name": "...", "description": "...", "supporting_skills": ["...", "..."], "confidence": "high|medium|low", "confidence_reason": "..."}
